Your everyday life

How it affects you.

Start with the situation, then distinguish the legal rule from our assessment of its consequences.

A platform steers you toward its own products or makes switching difficult.

The law

Sections 15 and 16 address anti-competitive agreements and abuse of dominance. Some provisions are broad enough in principle to reach restrictive data practices, self-preferencing, tying and limits on interoperability.

Our analysis · What this means for you

What you see and which services you can use may be shaped by platform power, not simply price. The law lacks clear digital-market standards for assessing these practices. A free service is not automatically anti-competitive; its effects on competition and consumers matter.

You use a platform without paying a subscription.

The law

The definitions of “relevant market” and “service” remain oriented toward traditional commercial exchanges, price, substitutability and geography.

Our analysis · What this means for you

Your attention, data and connections can still generate value and strengthen the platform’s position. Focusing too narrowly on price may miss that power, while treating every low-price service as predatory could wrongly discourage competition.

Markets, platforms and dominant businesses.

Our analysis

Where the framework falls short.

The Competition Act was designed around traditional markets. Its definitions and enforcement framework need updating to assess how data, attention, network effects and platform restrictions shape competition.

Platform power goes beyond price.

Self-preferencingTying and bundlingInteroperability blocksWalled gardens

The current framework does not adequately capture these forms of dominance.

Scope & Application

Section 22 allows the Competition Commission to enquire into overseas conduct that adversely affects the relevant market. However, explicit cross-border application and enforcement procedures remain unclear, creating uncertainty for offshore digital platforms.

The definition of a relevant market

'Relevant market' (s.2(s)) is price- and geography-centric — misses cross-border, multi-sided digital platforms

Services paid for with data and attention

'Service' (s.2(t)) misses zero-price/data models — where users pay with data and attention, not money

Abuse of dominance in digital markets

Some provisions of section 16 may cover restrictive data practices, self-preferencing and limits on interoperability in principle. The framework lacks sufficiently clear digital-market standards for assessing these practices consistently.

Institutional Structure

The qualification framework does not ensure that Competition Commission members have specialised digital-market expertise. We recommend technical expertise and continuing training to assess algorithms, platform economics and data-driven business models.

Our recommendation

Modernise.

We recommend modernising the framework for digital markets.
