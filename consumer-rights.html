Your everyday life

How it affects you.

Start with the situation, then distinguish the legal rule from our assessment of its consequences.

You exchange attention and data for a “free” service.

The law

The consumer definition assumes a purchase for consideration, and the service definition excludes services without direct financial consideration. These definitions fit conventional online purchases more readily than data- and attention-funded platforms.

Our analysis · What this means for you

You can contribute value without paying money, yet fall outside effective consumer protection for that exchange. This is a gap in this statute’s framework, not a claim that all other protections disappear.

A platform makes it easy to subscribe and difficult to cancel.

The law

The anti-consumer-practice provisions focus on conventional harms such as defective goods, misleading advertising, pricing irregularities and failures to provide services.

Our analysis · What this means for you

Confusing consent buttons, obstructive cancellation and misleading subscriptions can undermine informed choice. These digital design practices are not adequately addressed by the existing framework. Explicit protections against them are part of our proposed reform.

Our analysis

Where the framework falls short.

The Consumer Rights Protection Act focuses on conventional transactions. It inadequately addresses digital harms such as manipulative interfaces, profiling and personalised pricing, especially where users exchange data and attention instead of money.

“Free” services still receive something from you.

Consumer harm can be built into design.

The anti-consumer-practice definition misses contemporary digital harms.

Scope & Application

The Act does not clearly establish whether its obligations can be enforced against offshore platforms serving people in Bangladesh. This creates uncertainty in cross-border transactions, rather than a categorical exemption for every overseas provider.

Who counts as a consumer

'Consumer' (s.2(19)) assumes a paying buyer — misses 'free' services where users pay with data and attention

Services without direct payment

Section 2(22) excludes services provided without direct financial consideration. That leaves significant gaps for data- and attention-funded services, even though users contribute value.

Digital harms and manipulative design

'Anti-consumer practice' (s.2(20)) misses digital harms — dark patterns, algorithmic bias, manipulative recommendations, surveillance pricing
