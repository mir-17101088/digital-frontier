Consumers, online shopping and services.

How it affects you.

You exchange attention and data for a “free” service.

What the law says

The consumer definition assumes a purchase for consideration, and the service definition excludes services with no direct payment.§§2(19), 2(22)

What it protects

  • The consumer definition works for conventional e-commerce, such as Daraz or Foodpanda.§2(19)

Where it falls short

  • You can contribute value without paying money, yet fall outside effective consumer protection for that exchange.
  • This is a gap in this statute’s framework, not a claim that all other protections disappear.

A platform makes it easy to subscribe and difficult to cancel.

What the law says

The Act’s list of anti-consumer practices was written for conventional commerce.§2(20)

What it protects

  • Defective goods, misleading ads, pricing irregularities and failed services are prohibited practices.§§44, 45

Where it falls short

  • Confusing consent buttons, obstructive cancellation and misleading subscriptions can undermine informed choice.
  • These design practices are not adequately addressed by the existing framework.

A seller disappears after you pay.

What the law says

Failing to provide a paid-for service is an anti-consumer practice under the Act.§§1, 45

What it protects

  • The consumer directorate’s Director General holds an enforcement mandate.§21

Where it falls short

  • The Act does not define its territorial reach, so an offshore seller may fall outside it.

Analysis

Where the framework falls short.

The Consumer Rights Protection Act focuses on conventional transactions. It inadequately addresses digital harms such as manipulative interfaces, profiling and personalized pricing, especially where users exchange data and attention instead of money.

Undefined

Whether the law reaches offshore sellers

§1

The Act does not clearly establish whether its obligations can be enforced against offshore platforms serving people in Bangladesh. This creates uncertainty in cross-border transactions, rather than a categorical exemption for every overseas provider.

Undefined

Built for paying buyers

§§2(3), 2(19), 2(22)

The consumer definition assumes a paying buyer, which misses “free” services where users pay with data and attention.

The service definition excludes services provided without direct financial consideration. That leaves significant gaps for data- and attention-funded services, even though users contribute value.

The list of anti-consumer practices misses digital harms such as dark patterns, algorithmic bias, manipulative recommendations and surveillance pricing.

Recommendation

Modernize.

Consumer protection for the digital economy

We recommend substantially modernizing the Act: recognize data and attention as consideration, regulate manipulative platform practices, clarify its reach to offshore providers, and let the directorate issue rules for new harms.
