How it affects you.

You keep consensual adult intimate material privately.

What the law says

The Act broadly prohibits producing, storing, possessing and distributing pornography, with no exclusion for consensual adult material kept for private use, and gives search and seizure powers.§§4, 6, 8(7)

Where it falls short

  • Private material and devices can come within enforcement, not only public distribution.
  • Vague definitions and narrow exemptions create uncertainty for sexual-health education, artistic work and other legitimate expression.

An allegation involves intimate-image abuse or child sexual abuse material.

What the law says

The Act overlaps with the cyber law’s sexual-harm offense but carries a different penalty framework. Its offenses are cognizable and non-bailable: police can arrest without a warrant, and bail depends on judicial discretion.§10

What it protects

  • Penalties are graded, with heavier punishment for child pornography than for adult material.§8

Where it falls short

  • The legal route can change the penalties and the arrest and bail rules for the same conduct.
  • “Non-bailable” does not mean bail is impossible.
  • Failing to separate consensual content from exploitation weakens a focused response to serious sexual harms.

You look for sexual-health or educational material.

What the law says

The Act defines pornography through four adjectives, from “obscene” to “sexually arousing,” and also covers “soft versions.”§2(c)

What it protects

  • Religious materials are exempt, as are artistic and educational works.§9

Where it falls short

  • None of those terms is defined, so discussion of sexual health, education and rights can be caught.

What the Act prohibits

§§4, 6, 8(7)

The Act bans producing, storing, possessing, marketing, transporting, supplying, buying, selling or displaying pornography, and abetting any of these, and gives broad search and seizure powers.

Analysis

Where the framework falls short.

A vague, overbroad ban that fails to separate consensual adult content from real harms (CSAM, TFSV, non-consensual/deepfake); overlaps the Cyber Protection Act

Undefined

How “pornography” is defined

§2(c)

The definition rests on subjective terms that the Act does not explain, which invites arbitrary enforcement and overreach.

“Sexually suggestive”“Obscene”“Semi-nude”“Sexually arousing”

Too wide

Exemptions that leave out research and health

§9

The exemptions leave out research, medical, scientific, political and media-reporting purposes.

Bundled

Production and coercion at one tariff

§8

Producing pornography and coercing a person, including a child, can draw the same seven to ten years.

Doubled

The same conduct, two statutes

“Revenge porn” and child sexual abuse material are punishable under this Act and under the cyber law, with materially different penalties.

Our proposed wording (excerpt)

"pornography" means any material or content, in any medium, that expressly and predominantly depicts or describes, of or related to a person, any real or simulated sexually explicit acts ... which lacks significant literary, research, artistic, political, cultural, historical, religious, educational, media reporting, law enforcement or criminal investigation, medical, or scientific value or purpose ... but excludes child sexual abuse material, non-consensual pornography, or digitally created pornography
