Speech offenses and criminal defamation.

How it affects you.

You criticize public policy or encourage political participation online.

The speech offenses cover sedition, religious feelings, public order and related expression. The law also criminalizes inducing, or attempting to induce, students into political activities that may disturb public order.§§124A, 153B

What it protects

  • Criticizing government measures or actions is not sedition, so long as it does not stir up hatred, contempt or disaffection.§124A
  • Students themselves are not barred from political activity or discourse.§153B

Where it falls short

  • Broad terms make the boundary between lawful discussion and criminal conduct difficult to predict.
  • These exceptions do not remove the threat of criminal proceedings, nor do they bar civil lawsuits.

Someone alleges that your statement harms their reputation.

Criminal defamation carries up to two years’ imprisonment and/or a fine.§§499, 500

What it protects

  • Criminal defamation carries ten exceptions, including truth for the public good and comment on public servants.§499

Where it falls short

  • Those exceptions do not remove the threat of criminal proceedings.
  • Their interpretation varies, which leaves people discussing matters of public interest uncertain.

You run a promotion, giveaway or discount.

Offering a prize or reward to promote a business or to get people to buy something is an offense, with up to six months’ imprisonment and a fine of an unspecified amount.§294B

Where it falls short

  • Its wording could reach discount codes, cashback, loyalty programs, in-game rewards and other offers that are part of modern commercial practices.
  • That creates uncertainty for ordinary businesses.

The colonial speech offenses still in force

The law criminalizes sedition, promoting enmity between classes, wounding religious feelings, offenses against the state and public order, and inducing students into politics.

Analysis

Where the framework falls short.

Colonial-era speech crimes now used against online expression; overbroad, vague, disproportionate

Undefined

Offenses built on undefined words

Words such as “enmity,” “hatred” and “wounding” religious feelings carry no statutory definition, and “disaffection” is explained only as including “disloyalty and all feelings of enmity.”

Too wide

A low threshold, a severe ceiling

A mere attempt or likelihood can amount to an offense, with penalties up to life imprisonment.

Undefined

A standard built on “modesty”

§509

“Modesty” and “insult” are undefined and rooted in a colonial-era idea of morality, which invites subjective enforcement and fits contemporary harassment poorly.

Too wide

Everyday promotions within reach

§294B

In practice it could reach discount codes, cashback offers, loyalty programs, in-game rewards and virtual currencies, now ordinary parts of digital business.

Recommendation

Repeal speech offenses.

Targeted laws for specific harms

The listed speech offenses should be repealed and replaced with targeted, rights-respecting laws for specific harms.

Replace criminal defamation with civil remedies

Defamation should be decriminalized and replaced with civil remedies.

How others do it

Sedition and incitement elsewhere

Referenced to show different approaches, not as models for adoption.

United Kingdom

Coroners and Justice Act 2009

Abolished sedition and seditious libel in England and Wales and in Northern Ireland.

New Zealand and Singapore

Crimes (Repeal of Seditious Offences) Amendment Act 2007 · Sedition (Repeal) Act 2021

Repealed sedition and moved to laws aimed at specific harms such as terrorism or hate speech.

Read the text

Ireland, Denmark, Iceland and Canada

Repeal of blasphemy offenses

Removed blasphemy offenses as incompatible with freedom of expression in pluralistic societies.

Counter-example · India and Australia

Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 · National Security Legislation Amendment Act 2010

Kept the substance of sedition-type offenses under new names. These are poor examples to follow.
