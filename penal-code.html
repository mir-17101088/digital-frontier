Your everyday life

How it affects you.

Start with the situation, then distinguish the legal rule from our assessment of its consequences.

You criticise public policy or encourage political participation online.

The law

The speech provisions address sedition, religious feelings, public order and related expression. Section 153B also criminalises inducing or attempting to induce students into political activities that may disturb or undermine public order.

Our analysis · What this means for you

Broad terms make the boundary between lawful discussion and criminal conduct difficult to predict. The student provision targets encouraging participation, rather than imposing a blanket ban on students taking part in politics; that distinction is essential to understanding its effect.

Someone alleges that your statement harms their reputation.

The law

Sections 499 and 500 provide for criminal defamation, with up to two years’ imprisonment and/or a fine. Section 499 includes ten exceptions, including truth for the public good and commentary on public servants’ conduct.

Our analysis · What this means for you

Those exceptions do not remove the threat of criminal proceedings. Their interpretation can vary, creating uncertainty for people discussing matters of public interest. We recommend civil remedies in place of criminal defamation; that is a reform proposal.

Speech-Related Offenses

Speech offences: sedition (123A,124A); enmity between classes (153A,505(c),505(d)); wounding religious feelings (295A,298); acts vs the state (505(b),505A); inducing students into politics (153B)

Our analysis

Where the framework falls short.

Colonial-era speech crimes now used against online expression; overbroad, vague, disproportionate

Section 509

Section 509 addresses insulting a woman’s “modesty”. Undefined terms such as “modesty” and “insult” make the boundary uncertain and inadequately address contemporary harassment. We recommend replacing the provision with a comprehensive, inclusive harassment law.

Non-Speech Offenses

Section 294B criminalises offering prizes or rewards. Its broad wording could extend to ordinary digital promotions, such as discount codes, cashback or loyalty programmes, creating uncertainty for legitimate businesses.

Our recommendation

Repeal speech offences.

Targeted laws for specific harms

repeal the listed speech offences; decriminalise; replace with targeted, rights-respecting laws
