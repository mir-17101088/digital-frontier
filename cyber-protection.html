Your everyday life

How it affects you.

Start with the situation, then distinguish the legal rule from our assessment of its consequences.

A complaint is made about something you posted.

The law

Sections 25 and 26 cover specified sexual harms, blackmail and identity-related hate speech. Section 25 permits arrest without a warrant; section 26 requires a warrant. Both are bailable as a matter of right.

Our analysis · What this means for you

The distinction matters before a case reaches trial. Vague offence definitions and inconsistent observance of arrest safeguards can expose people to detention and legal costs even when a complaint lacks merit. Posting online is not, by itself, an offence.

Your post is removed or blocked.

The law

Section 8 allows removal before judicial authorization, followed by tribunal approval within three days. It requires restoration if approval is not obtained within that time.

Our analysis · What this means for you

Your content can disappear before a tribunal reviews the decision. The framework does not clearly explain how you are notified, how you can challenge removal, or how restoration is enforced. It also lacks an avenue for users to report harmful content under this procedure.

An investigation reaches your phone or computer.

The law

Sections 33–35 give investigators powers to search, seize digital evidence and devices, and arrest, including without a warrant in the circumstances covered by the provisions.

Our analysis · What this means for you

Access to a device can expose personal or business information unrelated to the alleged offence. The framework lacks sufficiently clear rules for limiting access, securing the data, and returning or destroying what was seized.

Sexual harms and blackmail · Section 25

s.25 criminalises content (incl. AI-made) for sexual harassment, 'revenge porn', CSAM, sextortion, blackmail

Non-Speech Offenses

Non-speech offences: illegal access (17,18), phishing/malware/ransom (19), gambling (20), forgery (21), cyber terrorism (23), unauthorised e-transaction (24)

Privacy

Officers can seize devices and arrest, often without a warrant (ss.33-35)

ss.38, 39, 43

Section 43 permits admission of evidence obtained under the statute notwithstanding contrary provisions in the Evidence Act, 1872 or other laws.

s.29

Section 29 places a burden on company officials to show that the offence occurred without their knowledge or that they exercised due diligence to prevent it.

s.50

Section 50 repeals the Cyber Security Act, 2023 and sets aside speech-related cases under that law and the Digital Security Act, 2018, while retaining non-speech cases. It does not clarify the status of cases under the Information and Communication Technology Act, 2006.

The agency’s independence

The cyber agency (NCPA) sits under a government ministry division, not independent

Identity information and hatred · Section 26

Section 26 addresses use or transmission of identity information for religious disharmony or communal or ethnic hatred. The analysis explains that the communication must lead to violence, fear or disorder; these terms and the causal threshold remain unclear.

s.8

s.8 gives an executive-led takedown power with no user-reporting channel and no prior judicial check

Non-Speech Offenses

The illegal-access provisions could expose good-faith security researchers to criminal liability. Identifying a vulnerability to improve security is not clearly distinguished from malicious access. This is a risk identified in our analysis, not a report that the researcher was prosecuted.

The undefined scope of gambling

'Gambling' (s.20) is undefined, risking capture of esports, fantasy sports, promotions
