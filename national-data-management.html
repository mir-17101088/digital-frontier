Your everyday life

How it affects you.

Start with the situation, then distinguish the legal rule from our assessment of its consequences.

Your identity records become connected across public services.

The law

Sections 30(6) and 32 establish mandatory database integration with the National Responsible Data Exchange. The framework covers records such as national ID, passport, tax and birth registration, and allows compulsory storage in national data centres where deemed expedient.

Our analysis · What this means for you

Connected records can improve service delivery. They can also make it easier to build detailed profiles across different parts of your life. A breach or misuse of access could expose linked information, while safeguards against unrelated reuse remain limited.

You need the data regulator to scrutinise a public body.

The law

The National Data Management Authority oversees both public- and private-sector processing. Government-led appointment, funding and administrative arrangements shape how it operates, despite its statutory description as independent.

Our analysis · What this means for you

An authority that depends on the executive may face difficulty scrutinising state data use impartially. Combined with data-protection exemptions, this creates a risk of stronger scrutiny of businesses than of public bodies holding your information.

An open-ended category · Section 31

Section 31 lets the government classify and restrict the exchange of “nationally important / sensitive data”. Open-ended terms and limited review safeguards create uncertainty about how broadly those powers may be used. We recommend deleting the provision in its current form.
