Speech & participation

You post something online.

A complaint, an arrest and a conviction are different stages. The law matters at each one.

Understand this situation
The rule and its limits

Under the Cyber Protection Act, section 25 allows arrest without a warrant; section 26 does not. Both are bailable. Separately, the Penal Code retains speech offences that can reach online criticism and political discussion. The particular allegation determines which rules apply.

Arrest and bail under the cyber lawPolitical expression under the Penal Code
Data & public services

An organisation holds your data.

Having data rights and controlling every use of your data are not the same thing.

Understand this situation
The rule and its limits

The Personal Data Protection Act recognises rights such as correction and deletion, alongside grounds for processing without consent. A separate law connects foundational government databases. Read the first to understand your control over data use, and the second to understand what connecting your records makes possible.

Consent, correction and deletionWhat linking public records changes
Consumer choice

You use a “free” service.

No subscription fee does not mean there is no exchange of value.

Understand this situation
The rule and its limits

Platforms can earn from your data and attention. Consumer law’s payment-based definitions may leave that relationship outside effective protection. Competition law asks a different question: whether platform power, restrictive terms or limits on switching undermine choice and fair competition.

The protection gap for free servicesHow platform power affects your choices
Content decisions

Your content is taken down.

Who ordered removal, and when does a tribunal review it?

Understand this situation
The rule and its limits

The Cyber Protection Act allows executive-led removal before tribunal approval, which must follow within three days. Restoration is required if approval is not obtained, but the process for challenging removal is unclear. We propose a notice-and-action framework with reasons, appeals and safeguards for users.
